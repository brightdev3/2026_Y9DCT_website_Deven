Examples of class work

What might learning actually look like? Here are some simple examples of the kinds of tasks students could do. These are illustrative examples, not actual Macleans assignments.

Mathematics: solving an equation

If 2x + 3 = 11, subtract 3 from both sides and then divide by 2. The answer is x = 4. An extension task might ask students to explain a method or solve a more complex problem.

Science: planning an investigation

Students might investigate how light affects plant growth. They could make a prediction, identify variables, record observations and explain their results.

English: responding to a text

A class could read a short story and write a paragraph explaining how the author develops a character, using a quotation as evidence.

Social sciences: exploring a place

Students might use a map to study how a community uses its local environment, then compare different points of view about a change to that area.

For current courses and official examples, check the school's subject guide.
